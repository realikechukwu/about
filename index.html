01 / Clinote

From a description to a structured log.

Recording clinical experience is easier when clinicians can describe what happened in their own words. Clinote helps turn that description into an entry they can check and save.

50 users

My contribution

I built Clinote end to end with Next.js, FastAPI and PostgreSQL. I integrated the OpenAI API and refined the prompt over multiple iterations to improve consistency with the required output structure.

I use AI-assisted coding and take responsibility for the implementation and product decisions.

A workflow with clinician review

The AI produces a draft. The clinician checks and edits it before saving. Clinote is intended for anonymised professional training logs, with instructions to exclude patient identifiers.

Prompt iteration is practical development evidence. I do not present it as a scored benchmark or clinical validation study.

02 / Cardiology Research Digest

A route from the literature to the inbox.

Keeping up with cardiology research competes with clinical work and training. I developed an automated digest to bring literature discovery and AI-assisted summaries into one email workflow.

70 subscribers

The implementation

The pipeline uses Python to retrieve literature from PubMed, filter and track article identifiers, generate abstract summaries through the OpenAI API, and deliver the digest by email.

GitHub Actions schedules the workflow and the digest is delivered by email. The source code and subscriber records are private.

The clinical judgement

A summary can help a reader find a paper. Assessing its methods, limitations and applicability still requires the original research.

Summaries include possible practice implications that readers should check against the original study. The digest supports literature discovery; it does not independently validate study quality or provide personalised clinical advice.
