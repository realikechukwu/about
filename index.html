Logging and training

Clinote: a logbook you can talk to

You describe a procedure or a case in your own words. Clinote turns it into a structured logbook entry. You read it, fix anything that's wrong, and save it. Nothing is saved until you've checked it. I designed and built it end to end.

Next.js, FastAPI, PostgreSQL, OpenAI API · 50 users as of October 2026 · anonymised training logs only

clinote.co · watch the demo · how it works

Also under Clinote:

  • Echo Companion: a scan checklist on your phone while you do an echo, then a structured report at the workstation, with BSE normal ranges and pathways built in. Nothing you enter leaves the browser tab.
  • Handover Teaching Wheel: spin it at the end of handover for a 90-second acute medicine teaching point.
  • Systematic review webinar: a free two-hour session on doing a systematic review, September 2026.

Keeping up with the evidence

MedPally: research, easier to follow

MedPally grew out of the Cardiology Digest. You pick a specialty and the journals you care about, and it gives you a personal feed of new papers, each with a short summary. It pulls from PubMed on a schedule and works out which papers are relevant to you. It starts with cardiology, but adding a specialty is a data change, not new code.

Django, htmx, PubMed, LLM summaries · source code public to read

app.medpally.com · medpally.com

The Cardiology Digest: where it started

An email that goes out every Sunday with the week's new cardiology papers worth reading. It checks about 20 journals, filters on the strength of the evidence, and summarises each paper in plain clinical terms. Summaries come from the abstract only, in the same format every time. Readers mark what was useful, and that shapes the next issue.

PubMed → evidence filter → LLM summary → email, on GitHub Actions · 70 subscribers

MedPally Find

A free, hand-curated directory of tools, apps, courses and guidelines for cardiology and cardiac critical care. Anyone can suggest a resource, and every suggestion is reviewed before it goes up. The code is open source under the MIT licence.

Making sense of AI

AI in Cardiology Decoded

A free, plain-language guide to machine learning for clinicians. Every term gets one line, one picture, one cardiology example and one warning. There are 12 chapters, 11 animated explainers and 5 interactive labs, from a decision tree you can grow to a sensitivity, specificity and AUC explorer. It's an independent study companion to the 2026 ESC scientific statement on AI in cardiology for non-experts.

aicardio.medpally.com

Reviewing medical AI

From September 2025 to March 2026 I reviewed medical LLM outputs for Outlier / Scale AI. I graded answers for clinical accuracy and completeness, including cardiology and ECG reasoning, and wrote feedback for the annotators.

Related reading: Magnifica Humanitas in plain English, my summary of Pope Leo XIV's 2026 encyclical on the human person in the age of AI.

How I build things

  • The AI drafts and the clinician decides. Every Clinote entry is read by a person before it's saved.
  • Stay close to the source. Digest summaries use the abstract only, in a fixed format.
  • No patient identifiers. Clinote is for anonymised training logs, and Echo Companion stores nothing.
  • I use AI-assisted coding, and I'm responsible for every decision it touches.
